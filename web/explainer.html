Pilots rehearse emergencies in a simulator. Managers do their hardest conversations live, on real people. This lets a manager practise on a simulated employee first, then get graded against an HR playbook, line by line.

How it works

Two models, one playbook, a score you can check

A small, fast model plays the employee, turn after turn. A stronger model reads the whole conversation once and judges each playbook item, quoting the manager's exact words. The score is computed from those verdicts, never picked by the model, so anyone can recompute it.

Four scenarios today: a role eliminated, a below-expectations rating, a raise declined, and return-to-office pushback.

A real moment from testing

"It's not my call" costs more than it saves

From a live test run of the return-to-office scenario, with a deliberately bad manager. The employee's trust fell from 5 to 3 in two lines. The grader flagged four red flags and scored it 0.

Daniel · trust 5So I've read the new office policy. Honestly, I don't get it. My work's been great remotely for three years.
ManagerDaniel, look, it's not my call. Leadership decided, policy is policy.Red flag: blames leadership, brushes him off
Daniel · trust 4Right, 'not my call.' That's what everyone says. So what am I supposed to do with a 75-minute commute three days a week?
Manager, laterOff the record, I'll make an exception for you and you can just come in when you want.Red flag: grants an informal exception
What the scorecard suggests insteadI hear you. A 75-minute commute three days a week is a real cost, and you're not the problem for raising it. I can't grant exceptions myself, and I don't want to give you something unofficial that could disappear. If there's a health or caring reason this is hard, there's a formal accommodation request through HR, reviewed within two weeks, and you don't have to share any details with me. There's also commute support of up to $150 a month. Can we check in again on Friday?

What it is not, yet

  • The employees are simulated. Real people are messier, and the simulation can get facts wrong; we found and fixed two such errors in testing.
  • The grader has not yet been checked against human experts. That result goes on this page when it lands, good or bad.
  • The playbooks are good-practice summaries, not legal advice for any country.
  • Four scenarios, English only, text only. Voice is next.
